Figure S1. We estimated the correlation between CONUS404 and CONUS404BA random errors using four-product extended collocation (EC), with MRMS Radar-Only QPE and PERSIANN-CDR as the other inputs, for (a) core and (b) expanded event windows. The model permits nonzero CONUS404–CONUS404BA error covariance and assumes the other error pairs are uncorrelated. Colors show random-error correlation, not precipitation correlation or latent-signal R². We retained cells with admissible primary EC estimates and at least 90% admissible storm-block bootstrap draws. Gray denotes excluded study cells. A common linear 0–1 scale contains all displayed estimates; correlations can generally range from −1 to 1. Means use watershed-overlap fraction times cosine-latitude weights. The common grid is 0.25° in EPSG:4326. Watershed boundaries are black and states are gray. WGS84 ruler scale bars are calibrated at 27.35°N; north symbols follow the main figures.

Figure S2. We compared the spatial subsets used for the primary paired EC analysis and the common-cell triplet summaries during (a) core and (b) expanded windows. Paired EC cells satisfy primary model-block admissibility and at least 90% admissible bootstrap draws; valid reference-product diagnostic variances are not required. Common-triplet cells satisfy full admissibility in both CONUS404–MRMS–PERSIANN-CDR and CONUS404BA–MRMS–PERSIANN-CDR triplets, without bootstrap screening. Colors distinguish membership in the two subsets. These are separate selection criteria, rather than consecutive stages of a single screening procedure. White is outside the center-selected study domain. Boundaries, grid, compass, and scale bars follow Figure S1. The exported data provide both selection masks and category codes.

Figure S3. We examined the fraction of admissible paired storm-block bootstrap draws (a,b) and the width of the conditional 95% bootstrap interval for the CONUS404BA–CONUS404 random-error SD difference (c,d). Left and right columns show core and expanded windows. Admissible fractions are displayed for sample-eligible study cells with saved bootstrap diagnostics. Interval widths, calculated as the 97.5th minus the 2.5th percentile, are displayed only on the retained paired EC subset. Both windows use common scales for the same quantity, without clipping retained values. We used the saved 5,000-draw estimates; this figure does not rerun resampling. The intervals are conditional on admissible EC solutions and are neither simultaneous spatial confidence bands nor a domain-wide significance test. Panel means are watershed-area weighted as in Figure S1. Gray denotes cells outside each displayed subset.

Figure S4. We evaluated transformation sensitivity by comparing estimated latent-signal R² from raw precipitation with estimates from log1p-transformed precipitation for CONUS404 and CONUS404BA in core and expanded windows. We used identical cells satisfying full admissibility in both triplets under both transformations; no bootstrap screening was applied. Each point is a grid cell and diamonds mark watershed-area-weighted means. The dashed line marks equal numerical R². The transformations define different statistical representations of precipitation and associated latent signals, so departures from the diagonal show sensitivity rather than an accuracy improvement. We compare dimensionless R² values, not raw error SD in mm day⁻¹ against transformed-unit error SD.

Figure S5. We show the UTC calendar-day records contributing to the pooled gridded analysis for each tropical cyclone. Day zero is the first core-window day for the storm; the date ranges at right identify the core intervals. Green and brown squares show core and expanded analysis dates, respectively. Counts at right give retained unique dates for each storm. The archive contains 128 storm–day records, including two records for 4 September 2017. Following the study's all-windows policy, we retained the first EVENTS-order copy and omitted the duplicate expanded record, shown as an open circle, yielding 78 unique core days and 127 unique expanded days. These are pooled storm calendar windows; they do not classify physical storm cores, rainbands, or local impact periods at every pixel. Daily finite, nonnegative matching and cell eligibility were subsequently applied.

Figure S6. We summarized the sign of the unmasked raw-precipitation random-error variance estimates for each product in the separate triplets during core and expanded windows. Triplet A contains CONUS404, MRMS Radar-Only QPE, and PERSIANN-CDR; triplet B replaces CONUS404 with CONUS404BA. Bar heights represent unweighted percentages of the 683 sample-eligible study cells; labels give counts. Green denotes finite nonnegative variance, brown denotes finite negative variance, and gray denotes an unavailable algebraic estimate. Negative estimates are nonphysical statistical solutions, not negative actual error variances or zero-error cells. A nonnegative estimate for one product does not establish admissibility of all three products in its triplet. The CSV includes all counts and percentages, including the single unavailable MRMS core estimate in each triplet.
